# Ten-person usability pilot

**Human usability pilot: not run.** No participants, responses, success rates or market-demand claims are invented. Browser automation and device emulation are engineering checks, not human testing or physical-device verification.

## Recruitment and consent

Recruit ten consenting adult participants, ideally mixing technical and nontechnical backgrounds. Assign anonymous identifiers P01–P10. Explain that the interface, not the participant, is being evaluated; participation is voluntary and they may stop or skip any task. Obtain separate consent for any screen/audio recording. Store only the minimum anonymous notes needed for the pilot; agree a retention period and delete recordings afterward. Do not request sensitive personal information.

Use the same tested version and record its commit. Allow 25–35 minutes per participant. Have a desktop or laptop ready, then ask them to use their own phone if they consent. Record actual browser/device and connection conditions. Do not describe emulation as a phone test.

## Moderator script

“This is a map of ideas about possible machine intelligence. Please think aloud as you explore. We are testing whether the interface communicates clearly. I will avoid helping unless you ask or cannot continue.”

Present tasks one at a time, without pointing to controls or explaining the scientific distinction in advance:

1. Find Metacognition and explain its meaning in your own words.
2. Find Substrate Mobility and explain the difference between migration and remote control.
3. Locate the evidence section and identify whether a claim is observed, hypothetical, or awaiting source verification. Explain what the top-level badge refers to.
4. Isolate a concept, reset the scene, and share the view. Open the link in another session and compare the selected concept, layers, separation and camera.
5. Complete the same essential exploration on your phone: find a concept, read its distinctions, isolate/reset and share.

Start timing when each task is read. End when the participant says they have completed it or after a five-minute limit. Record all assistance, hesitation and errors before helping. If blocked, give a neutral prompt (“What would you try next?”); record any subsequent directed assistance separately. Do not code assisted completion as unassisted success.

## Interpretation rubric

- Metacognition: distinguishes useful error/confidence monitoring from self-description.
- Substrate mobility: describes execution/state transfer, distinguishes it from issuing remote commands, and recognises dependencies.
- Evidence: recognises the future-hypothesis badge is not a present-system score; can identify scope and source-review status.
- Interaction: completes isolation and reset without losing the next task; shared state matches across sessions.
- Mobile: can reach all card content and operate the controls on physical hardware without overlap or inaccessible targets.

Preserve participants' actual explanations; don't infer understanding from clicking the right button. Tally outcomes with denominators and distinguish unassisted, assisted, partial and not completed. Report recurring misconceptions and fixable failures. Treat ten participants as a formative pilot, not a representative survey or evidence of demand. Rerun the failed tasks after fixes using a clearly labelled follow-up round.
